# Build: the reggae style (stage 6, part 5, fourth of five)

You are adding a style to Ensemble, a generative backing band: text and a seed go into a pure engine and a performance comes out. Blues, jazz, funk, rock, disco and neo-soul exist; the first four were auditioned by the owner (a bassist and guitarist) and passed. Reggae is the fourth of five more. Rock, disco and neo-soul were built the same way you are about to build reggae, from `prompts/style-rock.md`, `prompts/style-disco.md` and `prompts/style-neosoul.md`; their sections in `DECISIONS.md` ("rock", "disco", "neo-soul", and the "checked and merged" section after each, which says what the main session changed) are the nearest thing to a worked example.

## What to build

1. **`packages/styles/src/reggae/`** (style id `reggae`, name "Reggae"): `index.ts`, `drums.ts`, `bass.ts`, `chords.ts`, `solo.ts`, `critic.ts`, and one line in `packages/styles/src/index.ts`. Written from the reggae notes. Where a note is marked solid, follow it; where it says unclear, decide and log. The chord seat is the guitar's skank (ST-reggae-18 to 21); the keyboard double and the organ bubble (ST-reggae-22, 23) are not part of this build.
2. **Sound**: packs `drum-kit`, `electric-bass` and `clean-guitar` (all exist), the alto sax as the default soloist (ST-reggae-35), a mix in the style's `sound` in the manner of the others, General MIDI programs for the export. A horn's scoop is not in the instrument model and is not played. Do not invent an instrument: no organ.
3. **Library**: five or six charts in `library/reggae-*.chart`, written in the slow count (ST-reggae-01), in the manner of the others (look at their headers and how plain they are): the forms a reggae rhythm section is asked for most (for example a two-chord vamp in a minor key, a one to four in major, a minor i to bVII, a three-chord tune with a five, a one-chord dub groove, and one song with an intro, verses, a chorus, a breakdown where drums and bass play alone, and an outro). Give the song chart an arrangement block that fits it (`docs/CHART_FORMAT.md` section 11): drum and bass alone is what the block is for. Generic progressions only: no chart may be a named song.
4. **The critic and the scorecard**: `reggae/critic.ts` with weights and targets from the notes' measured numbers (ST-reggae-05, ST-reggae-15, ST-reggae-17, ST-reggae-20, ST-reggae-21 and what the other styles' critics show is needed), then `pnpm -s ensemble score --style reggae --update` to accept `scorecard/reggae.json` once the numbers are where the notes say they should be. Say in your report what each target is and where the style sits against it.
5. **A golden take or two** for reggae, the way `test/goldens.ts` holds the others, with the line in `DECISIONS.md` that the rules ask for.

## The rules of this build

- **Styles are data.** If reggae cannot be written in the data as it stands, the engine is missing a concept. A small general concept you may add, saying in your report why it is general and not a special case for reggae. If it is not small (a new way of playing for a seat, a change to the planner), **stop and report what is missing** instead of building it: that is a design call for the main session. Things the notes ask for that may or may not exist yet, to find out early: a bar with no kick on the One and the drop on 3 (ST-reggae-05 to 07), one riddim chosen for a whole performance and never switched (ST-reggae-08), a bass that plays the One only where the drummer's kick does (ST-reggae-13: it has to hear the kick, as funk's bass does), a bass figure with a hole on the One that a change at the barline may keep (ST-reggae-14: every other style's bass arrives on a change), a chop that is always the hand's loudest stroke with nothing on 1 or 3 (ST-reggae-20), a soloist's phrase said again on new chords (ST-reggae-27), a section that may not cross the line between the one drop and the high riddims whatever the arc does (ST-reggae-33), an arc at half depth (ST-reggae-34), the stop as an ending (ST-reggae-32), and rules for other meters (ST-reggae-04). The fast count (ST-reggae-01: a chart written with the skank on every "and" plays at double the written tempo) is not part of this build: write the library in the slow count and say in your report what reading a fast-count chart would take.
- **The other six styles must not change.** Their goldens and scorecards must hold exactly. If an engine change of yours moves them, it was not general.
- **No new properties** unless reggae breaks a rule a musician would name and no existing property can see it; then one, stated as a rule about music, and say so (the drop on 3 in every bar and no kick on the One below the high level is the likely one). **Do not loosen an existing property to let the new style through** without saying exactly what you loosened and why in your report: the last build excused more than it needed to, and one of its "bugs in another style" was a test that said more than the engine's rule. If a property seems wrong for this style, say what the rule should be. The suite's budget grows with the styles: 16 seconds with six, 18 with seven (`scripts/budget.ts`, `docs/TESTING.md`), and it stands at about 13.4 with six, each style in a process of its own, side by side, on 8 cores. Measure `pnpm check` with reggae added and report the number. If it goes over 18, do not trim anything: report it.
- **You cannot hear.** Never say it sounds good. Print takes and read them (`pnpm -s ensemble render library/reggae-….chart --style reggae --seed 1 --out x.mid`, and whatever the other builds used to read a part as text), measure them against the notes, and report the measurements. The owner will listen on the app.
- **Levels.** `pnpm levels reggae` renders each seat alone and reads its loudness (it needs Google Chrome and ffmpeg; it serves from the repo, which works under `.work/` now). Set the style's mix so the whole band reads near the other styles (funk, rock and disco read -17.0 LUFS with everyone playing, neo-soul -17.7, blues and jazz -18.5; aim for -17 to -18 here) and the soloist about a decibel under the band, and report the numbers. ST-reggae-37's make-up gain belongs to the old engine's mixer; use it as a hint, not a number.
- The app picks the style up by itself (the Style control and the library's shelves read `STYLES`). `pnpm smoke` must still pass.

## How to check your work

- `pnpm check`, `pnpm test:full`, `pnpm score` and `pnpm smoke` must pass. If the suite goes over budget while you are running other things, that is contention: rerun it alone before believing it.
- Read at least a low, a mid and a high pass of each seat on two charts and compare them with the notes' grids, bar by bar, before you call a seat done.
- Never `pkill -f`. Never wrap node in `timeout`. In zsh, do not name a loop variable `path`. Stop any server you start by its port (`fuser -k <port>/tcp`), and use ports 8770 and 8771 only.
